## Main content
### Draft 1
I pulled roughly six steps from Streamlined ID. The first is to figure out who your stakeholders are. Next is to determine if educational options are right for what problem you are seeing. Then we can gather data about our learners using various methods. Then we can analyze the learner to develop a persona. Next we can analyze the context which helps us determine where the learner is and where they are going on their journey. Finally we can analyze content, which is where we figure out what we are going to teach and how to structure it.

So. I found six questions that you can ask yourself from Streamlined ID.

Is educational content appropriate? Say someone is struggling to care about geometry. It doesn't matter how much instructional material you give them. They're still not going to care.
What are the constraints, needs, and resources that can help you? In the case of the person not caring about geometry, how can we find out about their needs? It would be useful to collect resources too. Also knowing the constraints that the person might be going through might be useful.
How can you create a lean persona, a rough characterization, of the user? This helps to personalize the data. Now you are looking at Bob who likes cars and is intrinsically motivated to build his own engine. Not a collection of impersonal data.

Finally, what are you going to try and teach? What is important, how can you sequence it, and what's the scope? If you are trying to teach geometry, it might be counter productive to go on a detailed tangent on the philosophy of ethics.
